    Method

    When autumn happens. NASA's MODIS land-surface phenology product (MCD12Q2) gives, for every 500 m pixel and year since 2001, the day the canopy has lost half its seasonal greenness. I take the median over a 5 × 5 km window around each place.

    Timing model. Each place's typical date, shifted by how warm its September was: one slope, shared by every place, of about 1.8 days later per °C. I tested several candidates by leaving each year out. A cold-degree-day senescence model, August or late-summer temperature windows, and a linear trend all did no better than the typical date; September warmth did. Weather is ERA5 reanalysis via Open-Meteo.

    This season. Once September is over it is known. Before that, the rest of the month is filled in from each past year. The model's own out-of-sample errors set the range, so it reflects how good the model really is.

    From satellite to street. iNaturalist observers annotate trees as green, coloured or leafless. Aligning those observations on each year's green-down date places the colour window (turning → peak → bare) around it, and shows which species go early or late. Species with few observations fall back on horticultural timing, blended by sample size.
